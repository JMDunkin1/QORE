# NGAS All-Year Beta strategy audit — 2026-07-21

## Decision

Do not promote a return-optimized historical variant. Keep `ngas-all-year-beta` in `needs-validation` and keep paper/live routing disabled. No tested return change could be defended below the requested 33% overfit-probability ceiling; the best low-risk idea is frozen as a non-executable prospective shadow instead.

The checked-in strategy has useful development evidence, but it does not satisfy a defensible non-overfit promotion standard. Its pre-holdout bootstrap is not significant, its train drawdown breaches the promotion floor, and the Winter component has no eligible candidate. More importantly, repository development began after the dates labeled as component holdouts, and holdout results were printed throughout repeated searches. The 2021-2026 history must therefore be treated as development-contaminated retrospective evidence rather than sealed out-of-sample evidence.

The 2026-07-22 second through fourth passes reached the same decision after testing source-universe stationarity, independent weather-episode attribution, physical weather-data integrity, execution-cost sensitivity, spatial-demand extensions, forecast revisions, exact target-weight combinations, a corrected Summer temperature statistic, and 2007-2019 GEFSv12 fixed-model reforecasts. The decisive third-pass finding is temporal: the checked Summer research compares a single 00Z temperature snapshot with a NASA POWER daily-mean normal. A causal four-sample target-local-day approximation materially improves forecast accuracy but removes the old Summer edge. The fourth pass then found that every one of 12 simple physical-demand-follow rules lost money in pre-2015 training and that the locked least-bad row failed the 2015-2016 validation gate, so the 2017-2019 holdout remained unopened. The legacy curve remains visible as development-contaminated research, but its Summer inputs are now categorically ineligible for paper/live execution. No return-optimized signal or sizing change is promoted.

## Fourth-pass fixed-model reforecast audit

The audit added a separate NOAA GEFSv12 fixed-model retrospective lane covering 3,865 daily 00Z control-member issues from 2007-04-18 through 2019-12-24. Each record uses bounded GRIB byte ranges for forecast hours 174, 180, 186, and 192, the exact reviewed 18-location basket, the retained NASA POWER normal, and source/index/range/normal/record digests. Thirteen yearly shards completed with zero failed records and merged to dataset digest `8a26357ea873b3239452909f863f46a54f360e2a89b76511522b00a6372d2f29`. NOAA describes this archive as a later fixed-model reforecast, not the forecast product historically available to a trader, so the result is a pseudo-out-of-sample physical-thesis test rather than an execution replay. Pre-2020 data also cannot reproduce the present QQQM fallback contract; the experiment therefore uses only a costed 35% UNG-versus-cash sleeve.

The preregistered family contains 12 simple Summer-hot/Winter-cold follow rules: anomaly thresholds 3F/5F/7F, breadth thresholds 25%/50%, and one- or three-session holds. Sizing is fixed at 35% and one-way cost at 3.2 bp. Training ends 2014; 2015-2016 is a confirmation gate; 2017-2019 is hidden and can be revealed only for the locked winner after aggregate and both individual validation years are positive, at least 20 validation sessions are active, validation Sharpe is positive, and the full-family five-session block-bootstrap p-value is at most 0.20.

Every candidate lost money in training. The least-bad and therefore locked row, `a7-b0p5-h1`, returned -3.28% with Sharpe -0.05 across 218 active sessions and 126 entries through 2014. It returned +3.01% in aggregate validation, but that split was +6.94% in 2015 and -3.67% in 2016; the full-family adjusted p-value was 0.6837. The validation gate failed. The code therefore left the 2017-2019 holdout unevaluated and did not create a holdout report. The hardened implementation's immutable lock digest is `720110d701ca92a4c61f5b42d1b911f5874cb9da05b195b68a640e8c0745b094`; the narrow versioned summary is `data/qore/research/gefs-v12-reforecast-audit.json`.

This older-regime result rejects the simple claim that a broad day-seven demand forecast by itself creates a reliable long-UNG edge. It does not test the current selector's price-confirmation, warm-short, reversion, storage, or index-fallback rules. It reinforces the decision not to promote a return change and provides an untouched 2017-2019 return window for a future, separately frozen hypothesis rather than spending that window after a failed validation.

The exact target-weight audit leaves only 177 of 4,115 rows after requiring positive train and 2024 increments, at least three positive selection years, and no more than two additional drawdown points. Only four also have a nonnegative 2025+ increment. The best of those raises the compounded headline to 305.20%, but its 2024 incremental daily-return sum is only 0.18 points, it changes four independently tunable legs, it lowers Sharpe from 1.3065 to 1.3016, and it slightly worsens drawdown. Selecting that row after observing 4,115 variants is well above the requested 33% overfit-risk ceiling.

The denser continuous audit is even more decisive. Of 83,951 unique vectors, 16,477 met train constraints and 1,408 of the protected shortlist passed 2024. Cost, perturbation, concentration, and block-bootstrap gates froze eight radical and eight ultra-near finalists before the script evaluated 2025 onward. All 16 trailed the baseline in both 2025 and the observed 2026 period. The radical finalists gained roughly 10.4-11.7 points through 2024, then lost 12.9-14.4 points in that within-run withheld slice and finished at only 280.42%-285.58%. The closest vector changed the six legs by only about 0.0%-2.5%; it gained 0.442 points through 2024 but lost 0.311 in 2025 and 0.082 in observed 2026. All eight ultra-near finalists lost 0.369-0.440 points in the combined report-only slice. Those failures put both radical and tiny changes qualitatively well above the requested 33% overfit-risk ceiling; assigning narrower numerical probability bands would be false precision because the finalists are correlated and the dates are development-contaminated.

The temporal correction is more important than any weight winner. Against load-weighted NASA POWER realized daily anomalies, GFS bias/RMSE improve from 3.516F/4.537F to 0.868F/2.803F; GEFS-mean improves from 5.061F/5.524F to 2.143F/2.896F. Combined bias falls from 4.289F to 1.505F, RMSE from 5.055F to 2.850F, R2 rises from -1.420 to 0.231, and directional accuracy rises from 75.59% to 80.87%. Yet the old and corrected event sets have only 0.185 Jaccard overlap. Under the unchanged warm-only Summer rules, heat-follow loses 3.41 points through 2024; the remaining 12.05-point reversion result comes from only 16 rows. Bias calibration fitted on train makes every tested 3F-8F threshold worse. This is measurement correction, not a parameter neighborhood that can be averaged away.

The hard 1.50% Summer threshold raised the exact all-year full return from 293.56% to 308.15% in the pre-remediation audit run, but it changed only three dates and the selection-prefix bootstrap remained weak at about p=0.125. The continuous ramp was safer: it raised that run to 301.15%, with a 28.60% CAGR, 1.324 Sharpe, and the same drawdown. It also survived stress costs. However, two pre-selection overnight gaps produced nearly all of that gain, the largest incremental UNG target was still 32.38% of deployable capital, and the unadjusted selection-prefix p-value remained about 0.134. A broader 1.50%-4.00% ramp lost to the current strategy. The narrow ramp therefore remains a prospective shadow, not an executed target.

The independent-episode Summer attribution is more informative than the headline component return. Heat-follow signals added 16.32 percentage points versus an identical index fallback across 52 independent issue episodes, but t=0.87 and the leg was negative in both 2023 (-2.93 points) and 2024 (-2.03 points). The fade leg added 21.73 points across 42 episodes with t=2.29 and positive marginal results in 2023, 2024, and 2025 (+6.85, +7.58, and +7.31 points). The physically appealing extreme-CDD subset showed +17.98 points across 13 episodes, t=3.03, and an 84.6% win rate, but it is tiny and was identified after observing the data. Replacing the existing trigger with that CDD rule reduced holdout edge to +2.49 points. The result supports prospective low-dimensional demand-feature research, not a historical trigger replacement.

The strongest second-pass Winter headline came from fixing both signal and heating-demand inputs to GFS/GEFS and weighting them equally. It raised the all-year full return from 293.02% to 318.00% and Sharpe from 1.307 to 1.375, while slightly worsening maximum drawdown from -28.34% to -28.71%. The chronological evidence rejects promotion: incremental net return was +6.936 points in training through 2024-03-31, -2.372 points in validation, +1.309 points in holdout, and -1.063 points across the combined post-train period; the latter was about 2.41 compounded-return points worse than the active comparator. Only 9 of 21 changed episodes were positive, the best episode supplied 80.9% of the total gain, and removing the top three changed +5.873 points to -4.429 points. Post-train block-bootstrap probability of a nonpositive increment was 55%-59%. Even the favorable full-history, incomplete five-challenger family adjustment produced p=0.4071, 0.3595, 0.3448, 0.3395, and 0.3112 at 1-, 5-, 10-, 20-, and 60-session blocks; this lower bound excludes the earlier 642-, 135-, and 2,025-candidate Winter searches. The fixed-core/equal hypothesis is therefore frozen only for deterministic research review.

Forecast revisions did not rescue the theory. Requiring day-7 confirmation of a causal day-10 forecast reduced selection-prefix return by 20.23 points; abstaining on disagreement reduced it by 14.94 points; continuous revision scaling reduced it by 4.85 points. Tuned 0.90x-1.25x multipliers produced apparent gains from only six or seven episodes, lost their edge when the largest adverse episode was removed, and had bootstrap probabilities of nonpositive mean around 33%-36% before a complete family adjustment.

The preregistered spatial summaries also failed. Load-weighted degree anomaly changed selection-prefix return by -31.71 points, breadth by -9.97, extreme-location count by -22.16, and the Texas freeze-off proxy by -1.46. A CNN, graph neural network, or learned volumetric heat map would have far more degrees of freedom than the roughly 52 independent Summer heat episodes and 126 usable Winter forecast atoms, so it cannot plausibly reduce overfit risk relative to these failed low-dimensional physical features. A Winter target-date/lead-alignment confirmation improved drawdown to -25.34% but lowered full return to 282.08%, Sharpe to 1.27, train return to 62.55%, 2024-2025 validation return to 99.64%, and the report-only return to 17.74%; the selection-prefix p-value worsened from 0.15414 to 0.19864. The same-direction Winter fade gate lowered full return to 259.18% and report-only return to 15.03%.

A naive 0.75% deadband appeared to add roughly 0.64 percentage points of full-period return. That result was invalid because it could suppress small UNG exits and paired index risk reductions. Under the corrected allocation-atomic policy, 0.75% instead changed full return by -0.187 points, validation by -0.104 points, retrospective evaluation by -0.045 points, and the 2025-onward slice by -0.175 points; it reduced turnover by only 0.742x and costs by about 0.011 points. A 1.00% deadband changed full return by -0.153 points and was also negative in recent, validation, and retrospective-evaluation slices while leaving nearly 1% per-leg and 2.37% whole-allocation residuals. Only the stress-cost full history favored 1.00%, by 0.107 points, while every validation, retrospective, and recent comparison remained negative. The production threshold therefore remains 0.25%. Exact threshold selection from this small, non-smooth effect would exceed the requested overfit tolerance.

The broad-search winners improve several retrospective metrics, but none clears a complete-family multiple-testing test. Every challenger was evaluated after the historical evaluation periods were visible. They are rejected for promotion.

The two target-sizing audits, checked component optimizers, all-year optimizer, overnight-policy audit, and frozen Winter shadow are repository-reproducible. The corrected-statistic 51,840-row Summer grid and the revision/spatial feature families were one-off exploratory runs over ignored local corrected-weather chunks. Their methods and input dimensions are recorded here, but their large intermediate calendars and dedicated runners are not versioned; those results may reject promotion or motivate a future preregistration, but they are not independently reproducible promotion evidence.

## Material audit findings

1. **Historical holdouts are not sealed.** Summer's labeled holdout begins 2025-01-01 and Winter's begins 2025-11-01, while repository strategy development first appears on 2026-06-11. Candidate reports disclose holdout metrics, so later family choices cannot be proven independent of them.
2. **The effective search universe is understated.** Current reality checks cover only the surviving families, not the historical architecture and parameter searches. The all-year family size of one also omits component selection.
3. **A latent Summer sizing path used same-day information.** Volatility-target candidates included the current close-to-close return in volatility used for the position effective at that day's open. The selected fixed-size candidate is unaffected, but the candidate path is noncausal until corrected.
4. **Risk-adjusted ranking was misstated.** Sortino used the standard deviation of negative observations instead of downside deviation over all sessions.
5. **Side gates do not isolate gas alpha.** Some thesis profitability checks credit blended portfolio/index return and can count a row in more than one Winter thesis. These gates should eventually use marginal net gas-sleeve contribution clustered by independent forecast episode.
6. **Summer 2026 evidence is missing, not flat.** The cooling-season day-7 GFS/GEFS calendars end 2025-09-30. The audited daily coverage contract finds 833 required issue dates, 758 fully covered dates, and a 75-date gap per source from 2026-04-24 through 2026-07-07. May-July 2026 rows silently fall back to the index, so the public curve contains no 2026 Summer overlays.
7. **Research/live target parity was broken in Winter.** The prior replay produced 15 mismatches in 1,362 rows, including sign-changing differences on 2024-11-25 and 2025-11-24. The causes were duplicated research/live target logic, a non-canonical storage-season boundary, and incomplete production-source provenance. AIGFS still lacks train/validation history, and research still assumes the adjusted open while live market orders necessarily arrive later.
8. **The broker boundary accepted excess UNG concentration.** Before remediation, a provenance-valid handoff could specify `abs(gasPosition)` up to 1 even though the reviewed seasonal contracts cap the Summer and Winter overlays far below that.
9. **The deadband could suppress safety actions.** Before remediation, the same generic threshold applied to routine drift and small UNG exits, direction changes, or exposure reductions. That made both research accounting and live reconciliation capable of preserving risk the target had explicitly removed.
10. **Short execution evidence is incomplete.** The baseline assumes zero borrow cost; live shortability checks help, but retrospective availability, rejects, and fills are not modeled.
11. **Two forecast atoms contained physically impossible temperatures.** All 18 GFS locations for issue 2022-11-30, target 2022-12-10, lead 10 were near -459F, producing a -495.807F weighted anomaly; all 18 GEFS-mean locations for issue 2026-02-12, target 2026-02-13, lead 1 were -459.67F, producing a -491.868F anomaly. These are missing/corrupt-data sentinels, not weather. They previously entered forecast scoring and weather QA. Removing the two atomic score rows and their 36 location rows corrects the joined weather-QA result to RMSE 5.63F and R2 0.374 over 18,366 observations.
12. **The Winter source score is non-stationary.** Thirty-one of 37 train follow events were formed mostly from GFS and GEFS, while validation and holdout events typically combined four to six sources; AIGFS has no train or validation history. Taking a maximum source score and increasing confidence with source count mechanically rewards later periods for having more models. This is why a fixed source core is a sensible research hypothesis, but its negative post-train result prevents promotion.
13. **The return evidence is concentrated and cost-sensitive.** The selection-prefix active-return sum is 24.878 points; the top day supplies 22%, the top three 59%, and the top five 89%, with only 2021 and 2023 contributing material edge. An illustrative 25 bps UNG/5 bps index cost model plus 10% borrow makes prefix annualized active edge -0.139%; 50/10 bps plus 20% borrow lowers it to -7.467%. Paper slippage and real short-borrow evidence are therefore economic gates, not operational formalities.
14. **A volumetric map is already approximated, but sample size limits the model class.** The reviewed 18-location basket preserves spatial breadth and load weights and derives weighted temperature anomaly, HDD/CDD demand, and extreme-location coverage. A next shadow may preregister a few low-dimensional features such as load-weighted HDD/CDD, breadth, and a physically defined freeze-off proxy. A CNN or other high-capacity map model is not justified by 52 independent Summer heat-follow episodes and would sharply raise overfit risk.
15. **Forward capture still lacks a trusted production writer.** Owner-only JSON, self-reported timestamps, and internal digests make accidental mutation visible but cannot prove that a commitment existed before the open or that its upstream weather and market inputs are authentic. The new gate therefore refuses to call production evidence pristine until a reviewed writer uses an internal New York clock, tight skew, atomic one-per-date creation, and exact live handoff/input bindings.
16. **The Summer shadow journal had the same wall-clock limitation.** Its append path rejected a supplied post-open `generatedAt`, but did not compare that caller-supplied value with its own wall clock. This is fixed: production append now uses its internal New York clock, enforces a 60-second skew cap and same-date/pre-open session checks, and permits a supplied clock only behind the explicit test capability. Shadow rows remain execution-ineligible and cannot satisfy the forward gate.
17. **Normalized paper rows are not independent broker attestations.** The verifier can recompute notional, spread, slippage, signed-position chains, and timing, but it does not independently authenticate order type, time-in-force, extended-hours flags, shortability, the initial position snapshot, or client-order provenance. Explicit live approval remains responsible for reviewing those broker facts.
18. **The Summer forecast statistic did not match its baseline statistic.** The legacy GFS/GEFS calendars use one instantaneous 00Z target snapshot while normals and realized observations use NASA POWER daily-mean T2M. The four-sample local-day approximation cuts combined RMSE from 5.055F to 2.850F and changes most events; the corrected signal has negative train and recent edge. Legacy Summer research is therefore display-only and execution-ineligible.
19. **Two Summer directions were dead or theory-incoherent.** The selected historical ledger contains zero `summer-cold-short` and zero Summer `reversion-long` rows. A Summer cold forecast is an opposing-demand veto, not positive evidence for a natural-gas short; after that path is removed, a long reversion is unreachable because the only Summer fade reverses a heat-long rally. Both tuples are removed from optimizers, live inference, caps, the exact target lattice, and broker acceptance.
20. **The remaining weight improvements are search-fragile.** Four of 4,115 chronologically constrained discrete weight rows stay nonnegative after 2025. The best raises the legacy headline to 305.20% but changes four legs, adds only 0.18 points in 2024, lowers Sharpe, worsens drawdown, and is negative in 2022 and observed 2026. A separate 83,951-vector continuous search froze 16 finalists; every radical and ultra-near finalist lost to baseline in both 2025 and the observed 2026 period. No weight change is defensible below a 33% overfit probability.
21. **Temporal labels alone did not authenticate Summer values.** A legacy hours-0 row could initially be relabeled as `6|12|18|24`. The corrected contract now binds the ordered per-offset values, valid times, forecast hours, source URL/index identity, fetched index and selected-field payload digests, the exact retained per-location normal payload, location vectors, and complete location set. Validation re-derives each normal from that retained reviewed payload. Relabeling, normal changes, duplicate/orphan groups, and resume-cache substitution fail closed.
22. **Caps alone did not prove an emitted target.** A within-cap UNG fraction could initially pass the handoff even when no reviewed rule could produce it. The executable component contract now seals the exact Summer target set and the parameters plus materialized set digest for Winter; the common verifier rejects any nonzero target outside that lattice at inference, evidence, readiness, and broker boundaries.
23. **A valid lattice point could be substituted after inference.** The signal handoff previously carried enough mutable target fields for one allowed Summer size to be swapped for another allowed size before broker use. It now binds a canonical digest and identity of the configured source-inference target. Readiness, initial broker preflight, and the final per-order mutation boundary independently reread that source snapshot, compare every target/provenance field, and fail if either file changes.
24. **The prospective protocol lacked an immutable preregistration identity.** A mutable `reviewedAt` could postdate the evidence, so date ordering alone could not prove that one candidate family and policy were frozen before collection. The manifest now has a separate `sealedAt`, a canonical preregistration digest, and a one-candidate frozen registry. Every forward artifact, writer journal, and commitment binds that identity; a same-day/post-start seal, candidate addition, registry/policy change, or pre-seal inference fails. Local hashes and timestamps establish internal consistency only—external chronology still requires a signed remote commit/tag, transparency log, or WORM/notary timestamp.
25. **An exact target could still claim the right component outside its active date policy.** Summer trades are keyed to the first market session after the forecast issue, so April 25-30 exposure for a lead-7 May demand target is genuinely reachable; ordinary Summer sessions remain May-September. April 24 is collection-only and cannot carry the resulting position. Winter material exposure is reachable from November 2 through March 31 because a November 1 issue cannot be entered until a later session. Before remediation, forged lattice-valid Winter targets on November 1, in April, or in October could pass the shared provenance boundary even though the engine could never emit them. The exact Summer session/lead/entry-lag rule and Winter entry-date rule are now part of the sealed component contract and are enforced by both generation and acceptance.
26. **A plausible score row was not cryptographically or mathematically tied to its location rows.** A caller could previously retain a score while substituting, omitting, duplicating, or reweighting Winter locations. Fresh executable inputs now require exactly the reviewed 18 location IDs and weights and recompute `locationCount`, `sampledWeight`, weighted anomaly, cold-coverage weight, and extreme count from the matching source/issue/target/lead/window/model location atom. Fresh rows require exact three-decimal agreement; a one-thousandth anomaly tolerance exists only for retained versioned Winter research rows, and a two-thousandth mismatch still fails.
